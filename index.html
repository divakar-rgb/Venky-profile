The Leap

After two decades inside the world's largest technology companies, Venky Karukuri made a deliberate choice: stop building inside other people's roadmaps, and start building toward one conviction - that groundbreaking, globally relevant technology can be built from anywhere, for everyone. He founded ZenV Quantum to build it.

That conviction is backed by more than 20 years building software, cybersecurity, and digital platforms at Broadcom, CA Technologies, Microsoft, and HCL Technologies, and two U.S. patents earned along the way. But the credentials were never the destination; they were preparation for what he would go on to build.

"My mission is to use everything technology has taught me—security for the world, opportunity for the next generation, and success that follows when purpose comes first."

The Conviction

Qudo and ZenCube are one conviction of his, expressed twice: technology should make the world more secure, and make opportunity less dependent on where you started.

01 · QUDO

Securing the Quantum Transition

Quantum computing will eventually break the cryptography the internet runs on today. Qudo is his answer to that deadline—a quantum-safe cybersecurity initiative spanning post-quantum cryptography, secure communications, and quantum random number generation, built as next-generation security infrastructure organizations can adopt before they're forced to.

02 · ZENCUBE

Forging Potential Into Industry-Ready Talent

The world graduates more engineers than ever and still faces a widening employability gap. He built ZenCube to close it: an industry-to-talent platform where students, institutions, and enterprises collaborate through real-world projects, internships, and capstone programs.

Beyond the Platforms

He's building the ecosystem, not just the company.

As Director of ZenV Foundation, his involvement doesn't stop at ZenV's own products. He actively supports educational institutions, startup founders, researchers, and industry leaders through collaborations designed to strengthen the global innovation ecosystem as a whole.

It's the same conviction that shapes both platforms: talent is universal, but opportunity isn't, yet. That's a divide he believes technology—and enough stubbornness—can close.
